# Findings

**A working record, not the memo.** The memo — situation, approach, findings, recommendation, caveats — is written once the findings are settled, together with `README.md` and the figure. This file exists so that a number found in September is still available in November, and so that every one of them carries the file that regenerates it.

Shares and counts below are over the whole range, 49,655 hours from 2021-01-01 to 2026-08-31, unless a line says otherwise. Anything touching price *levels* or extreme hours is affected by the 2021-2022 gas-market event recorded in `DATA.md`; anything touching the sign of the price is not.

## Q1_NEG_EXT — how often prices are negative or extreme, and when

Regenerated by `analysis/01_negative_and_extreme.sql`.

**Totals.** 1,970 negative hours, 3.97% of the range. 4,956 extreme hours, 9.98%. No hour is both.

**By hour of day.** Negatives peak at 13:00 at 15.71% of that hour's hours and fall to zero at 20:00. Extremes are the mirror: they peak at 19:00 at 17.25% and bottom at 13:00. The two are inverse across the whole day, not merely different.

**How deep, by hour of day.** The 5th percentile of the deepest quarter-hour crosses zero at 10:00, bottoms at 14:00 at −21.58 EUR/MWh and is back above zero by 17:00 — a seven-hour window in which the worst 5% of hours pay the consumer. Outside it the same statistic reaches +64.94 at 19:00: even the cheapest moments of the evening are expensive.

**By day of week.** Sunday 9.88% negative against 6.00% extreme; Wednesday 2.29% against 11.69%. The 5th percentile of depth splits the week at zero: Sunday −4.39, Saturday −0.23, every weekday between +7.73 and +21.87.

**By month.** Negatives peak in May at 8.71% and are near absent in February, 0.12%. Extremes appear to peak in December at 23.79% — but 844 of December's 885 extreme hours are in 2021-2022, and with those years removed every month falls between 0.10% and 1.84%. The monthly extreme profile is when the crisis happened, not what months do.

**By year.** Negatives rise monotonically as a share of hours: 0.80, 0.97, 3.61, 5.21, 6.76, 7.70%. Extremes do not follow: 7.32, 46.29, 0.54, 0.76, 0.91, 1.13%.

**By hour within month.** The sharpest cell is May at 14:00, 38.17% of its hours negative, with a *median* hourly price of 17.92 EUR/MWh. Seven of the top eight cells are months 4-6 crossed with hours 12-15.

**Author's reading.** The weekday/weekend split and the daily shape are consistent with consumer usage. The monthly shape was initially puzzling and resolved as an artefact of the crisis rather than a seasonal effect.

## Q2_MIX — how the generation mix shifts when prices go negative or extreme

Regenerated by `analysis/02_generation_mix.sql`.

**The shift over the whole range, in percentage points of median share.** In negative hours `B20` is +41.75 and `B04` is −22.76; `B05` −11.73, `B19` +1.42, `B16` +1.00, `B18` +0.24. In extreme hours the two largest reverse: `B04` +12.30, `B05` +7.95, `B20` −5.20, `B18` −5.16, `B19` −2.09.

**Grouped by year, three things appear that the pooled figure hides.**

*`B20`'s negative-hour shift grows.* 32.0, 35.2, 28.1, 40.2, 46.4, 48.7 from 2021 to 2026. The pooled +41.75 is an average over a trend. `B04` shows no trend across the same years, sitting between −16.9 and −23.3.

*The extreme-hour shift is understated by pooling, not overstated.* `B04` reads 10.0 and 8.7 in 2021-2022 but 26.7, 25.9, 27.5 and 22.3 from 2023. In 2022 nearly half of all hours were extreme, so "extreme hours" and "all hours" were close to the same population and the contrast collapsed. Separating the years roughly doubles it.

*Wind changes sign.* `B18` offshore runs +2.6 in 2021 to −2.5 and −3.8 in 2025-2026 on the negative side; `B19` onshore falls from +7.0 to +0.6. The pooled figures, +0.24 and +1.42, show neither.

**Who leads, by month.** `B04` leads eight months of twelve; `B20` leads March, April and May. Four of `B04`'s eight leads are inside 2.24 points — October 1.06, March 1.17 the other way, July 1.65, February 2.24 — against January 9.42 and November 8.94. So the two are close for about half the year and gas pulls away only in November to January.

**Third place.** `B05` holds third every month except May and June, where `B18` takes it at roughly half the share. The gap between second and third widens from 6-13 points to 23.91 and 25.99 in those two months.

**`B20` is not interpretable as a share**, and that is a result rather than a gap. Its share peaks in April-May at 33%, dips in August to 24.6% and is lowest in November at 22.0% — a curve matching neither solar nor heating. The two measurables that split it by behaviour do work: the daily swing runs 2,956 MW in January to 8,262 in June, and the daily floor runs 2,372 in February to 1,034 in August. They peak in opposite seasons.

**`B20` over time.** The swing more than doubles, 3,861 MW in 2021 to 8,062 in 2026; the floor steps once between 2022 and 2023, 1,439 to 1,980, and is flat afterwards. 2026 covers 243 days ending 31 August, so its swing excludes the four lowest months and is biased upward.

**Author's reading.** The shift is more pronounced on the negative side than the extreme side, for fossils and `B20` alike. `B20` remains hard to interpret directly.
